# Milestone 2: simulation port

Status: complete. 2 October 2026.

## What works

- **Parity with the original prototype, proved.** The tests run the prototype's own code in Node (`tests/parity/prototype-harness.mjs`, unchanged source) and compare the TypeScript port against it.
  - 32 cases: all 19 scenarios, plus chains covering growth, sunset with battery, the evening peak followed by N-1, relief actions, auto-balance, forced cooling, switching, fault clearing, recoupling and load shedding.
  - Each case is checked at six points in time.
  - Every compared field matches to within 1e-6: flows, directions, energisation, temperatures, trips, state of charge, frequency, mode and the supply and demand breakdown.
- **Engineering preset.** This is what the demo runs. The balance closes at every node at every step (residual under 1e-6 MW in 300 random states and in every scenario over four hours).
  - **Topology:** a sectionalised 220 kV busbar with a bus-section breaker; the border tie through T4 at 275 kV; bay devices with interlocks; switching programmes; protection lockout with confirmation by a person.
  - **Transformers:** IEC 60076-7 thermal model with cooling stages, forced cooling, oil-pump failure and relative ageing.
  - **Plant:** wind power curve with soft cut-out and restart; solar from the sun's position and cloud; battery energy with round-trip efficiency; gas start-up, minimum stable generation and ramp; transfer requests that take effect over about 10 minutes.
  - **Frequency:** a two-area model with real-time event transients (rate of change, nadir) alongside the quasi-steady value on the clock.
  - **Station condition:** Normal, Alert, Emergency, Supply lost, Restoring, plus the N-1 screen for the two 400/220 kV units.
- **Scenario claims are tested.** Highlights:
  - The baseline is N-1 secure at 124%.
  - The evening peak is flagged N-1 insecure at 164% before anything fails.
  - **Loss of T1 at baseline:** T2 settles at 116 to 120 °C and stays in service.
  - **Loss of T1 at the evening peak:** T2 alarms within 6 minutes and trips on winding temperature at about +33 minutes if nothing is done.
  - **Battery plus a transfer:** T2 survives.
  - **Bus fault:** only section B is lost, and re-energisation is blocked until confirmed.
  - **Pump failure:** T1 runs more than 10 K hotter than T2 within 15 minutes, while the temperature alarm takes about an hour.
  - **Storm:** T2 trips at +40 minutes, the wind farm cuts out at about +2 hours, and T1 then cascades.
  - **System split:** Northern Ireland falls at about 0.6 Hz/s.
- **Runtime.** The engine runs in a Web Worker on a fixed 5-second step with selectable compression and time-lapse fast-forward.
  - Commands return before-and-after deltas, showing the settled state when a change ramps in.
  - States can be rebuilt from the input log (replay test).
- **Single file, offline.** `npm run build` produces one 77 KB HTML file with the worker inlined. `npm run e2e` opens it from `file://` in headless Chromium, drives two scenarios and asserts no network requests and no errors.
  - Confirmed: a `file://` page is a secure context in Chromium and WebGPU is available there, which retires a plan risk.
- **Copy and provenance.**
  - A test scans every user-facing string (scenario copy, component names and notes, assumptions, toasts, every log line from a long story) for em and en dashes, emojis, US spellings and any mention of EirGrid.
  - Every figure has a source type.
  - `docs/METHOD.md` is generated from the assumptions table.

Test totals: 99 tests in 9 files, run with `npm test`.

## Departures from the plan, and why

1. **Fans switch on winding temperature, not top-oil.**
   - With top-oil control, T2 stayed on natural cooling for minutes after T1 tripped at the peak, and tripped in 6 minutes.
   - Large units commonly switch fans on the winding temperature indicator, which reacts in minutes.
   - The plan's section 4.2 said top-oil; this is the more typical and more defensible choice.
2. **Operator actions are treated as coordinated with the system.**
   - Battery dispatch, transfer requests, gas starts and load shedding are assumed to be balanced by the rest of the system with a lag.
   - Sudden steps of 10 MW or more (a battery step, a trip that loses load, a split) are still reported as frequency events with their transient.
   - Plan 4.4 listed load shedding and gas starts as frequency events. Here a gas ramp is too slow to register, while a large battery step does register.
3. **"Settled" deltas only count changes that complete within 30 minutes.** Weather trajectories, such as the storm's two-hour wind rise, are forecasts rather than the effect of an action.

## What is approximate

- **IEC 60076-7 values are from memory** and marked "to confirm" in the Method table:
  - thermal exponents and time constants;
  - the normal cyclic and emergency limits.

  I could not check them against the standard text from here. A transformer engineer at EY or the client should confirm them before the session.
- **Frequency parameters are assumptions** (kinetic energy, response, damping, restoration lag), chosen to give plausible magnitudes. They are not a dynamic study, and the Method panel says so.
- **Ambient temperature** is a simple daily curve for March.
- **Solar** uses a clear-sky gain and a cloud attenuation, not irradiance data.
- **Demand does not follow a daily profile.** Regional demand stays at its set-point until a scenario changes it, as in the prototype.
- **The station cannot export at baseline demand.** Even with everything built (wind 200 MW, solar about 100 MW at best in March, gas 180 MW, battery 100 MW), local generation is less than the 680 MW baseline demand. The prototype had the same limit. The guided tour's export moment (plan 5.4, beat 2) will need either a night-time demand level or a daily demand profile.
